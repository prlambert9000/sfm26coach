# Performance Nutrition & Metabolic Programming Guide

Pescatarian-aware nutritional framework for a 155 lb (70 kg) athlete with a 58 VO2 max, focused on structural repair, inflammatory management, and glycogen optimization for the SF Marathon 2026.

## I. Macronutrient Targets for High-Volume Training

| Nutrient | Daily Target | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 125–140 g | Eggs, dairy, seafood for leucine and recovery |
| Carbohydrates | 350–500 g | Scale up during peak weeks; complex carbs and hydrogels |
| Fats | 60–80 g | Omega-3s (salmon, sardines) for joint inflammation |

## II. Timing the 6 AM Training Window

- **Pre-workout (5:30 AM):** Quick-digesting carbohydrate — half banana, small toast, or 20 g carb gel — to top off liver glycogen.
- **Immediate post-workout (7:15 AM):** 30 g+ high-leucine protein. Examples: 4 large eggs, or 1.5 cups Greek yogurt with berries.
- **Nightly repair:** 20 g casein-rich protein (cottage cheese or milk) before bed for overnight tissue synthesis.

## III. Pescatarian Anti-Inflammatory Protocol

To manage patellofemoral inflammation, dietary intervention targets systemic inflammation:

- **SMASH fish 3×/week:** Sardines, mackerel, anchovies, salmon, herring. High EPA/DHA dampens inflammation in the patellar fat pad.
- **Collagen co-factors:** High vitamin C (bell peppers, citrus, broccoli) alongside protein meals to support tendon health.
- **Hydration & electrolytes:** Aim for 1000 mg sodium/L during high-sweat 6 AM sessions. Magnesium-rich dairy to prevent cramping.

## IV. Gut Training for Race Day (70–80 g Carb/Hour target)

Goal: train the gut to comfortably absorb **70–80 g/hr** by peak weeks. **Target band revised down from 90 g/hr on June 7, 2026** — see rationale below.

**Race-day carb target — why 70–80, not 90 (set June 7, 2026):** The 90 g/hr ceiling is real physiology — glucose/maltodextrin saturates one gut transporter (SGLT1) at ~60 g/hr, and fructose adds ~30 g/hr via a second (GLUT5), which is why PF30's 1:0.8 malto:fructose blend can exceed 60 cleanly. **But the dose-response is curved, not linear, and the marginal gain from 75→90 is small.** For a ~3.5-hr (3:30 goal) marathon: glycogen covers the first ~90–120 min, after which intake matters — but the big benefit is in the 30→60 jump (bonk insurance), a meaningful-but-smaller gain 60→75, and a marginal one 75→90 that mostly buys insurance for miles 22–26. For a 3:30 runner whose #1 constraint is **knee durability, not a podium**, forcing 90 isn't worth the GI gamble. **The real reason to push the higher end in training is to earn the *option*** — the gut adapts over weeks, so train ~75–80 until it's boring, then race-day sit comfortably at 70–80 with margin to dial up if the day feels great. Net: don't chase a precise number; the difference between 60 and 80 is seconds-to-a-couple-minutes and late-race comfort, not whether 3:30 happens. **90 g/hr stays only as an optional stretch if 25-min spacing feels effortless — never a requirement.**

**Chosen fueling system (locked June 7, 2026 — supersedes the May 14 Maurten lock):** **Precision Fuel & Hydration — PF 30 Gel.** Maurten never shipped in time to become the working system; PF30 was substituted at B2B (May 17) and has since become the actual race-fueling product Paul trains and races on. As of June 7 he is **fully committed to PF30** — treat it, not Maurten, as the locked system. PF30 gels tear reliably one-handed, GI tolerance has been clean across multiple rehearsals (B2B, June 7 long run), and the 30 g dose is a clean unit for every-30-min feeding. **Precision deliberately keeps the gels low-sodium and doses electrolytes separately** — so sodium continues to come from the LMNT in the handheld (~½ packet ≈ 500 mg per ~16–20 oz, targeting ~1000 mg sodium/L), never from the gel.

**Precision Fuel product lineup (locked):**

| Product | Serving | Carb | Sodium | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PF 30 Gel (Original) | 1 pouch (51 g / 1.8 oz) | 30 g | minimal (~16 mg) | Workhorse gel, 1:0.8 malto:fructose, non-caffeinated |
| PF 30 Caffeine Gel | 1 pouch | 30 g | minimal | + 100 mg caffeine — reserve for late-race kicks |

**Standing protocol (Paul's current method, June 7):** take **1 PF30 at T−5→0** (right before the start), then **1 PF30 every 30 min** (30, 60, 90, 120 …). That cadence = 2 gels/hr = **60 g/hr** as the working baseline. **Progression lever is simply gel spacing:** every 30 min = 60 g/hr, every 25 min = ~72 g/hr, every 20 min = 90 g/hr. Tighten the interval as the gut tolerates — no need for bigger gels.

- **Weeks 1–4 (40–50 g/hr):** ~1 PF30 every 35–40 min (early-build appetite ceiling — B2B confirmed ~50 g/hr was the race-effort limit).
- **Weeks 5–6 (60 g/hr):** the standing 30-min cadence. Confirm it's clean across full long runs before tightening.
- **Weeks 7–8 (~72 g/hr):** tighten the **back half** of the long run to every 25 min — test the higher end late in the run, where race-day GI is most likely to rebel.
- **Weeks 9–10 (race-day band, 70–80 g/hr):** hold ~25-min spacing (~72 g/hr) as the race-day plan, landing in the 70–80 band. **90 g/hr (every 20 min or a PF90) only if 25-min spacing already feels effortless** — optional stretch, not the goal.

**Fast-finish fueling rule (learned June 7):** when a long run has an embedded MP/threshold close, the back-half gel often comes due *during* the surge and is easy to skip under pace focus (Paul missed the 90-min gel on June 7 for exactly this reason — a focus call, not GI). **Pre-load it:** take the gel that would land mid-surge a few minutes early, *before* the fast finish begins, so the feeding is banked before attention narrows to pace.

### Presidio HM — the high-HR fueling problem (learned June 21, 2026 — THE binding constraint for the marathon)

The Presidio Half (Pivot test) exposed the single most important fueling fact of the build, and it is **not** a gut-tolerance problem — it's a **behavioral/appetite-at-effort** problem. **GI has never been the limiter; getting fuel *in* at race HR is.**

- **What happened:** plan was 3 carried / 2 taken (start + ~mi 6.5). **Actual = one gel, at ~65 min.** A **side stitch** (under the right ribs, on the Presidio→bridge descent — an ETAP-type stitch, NOT a muscle cramp; it resolved on its own) made him gun-shy about eating/drinking for the back half, which compounded the appetite suppression → a glycogen-flat final ~4 miles: *"tired, hard to push, almost sleepy, hard to stay motivated… not pain, just not wanting to push."* That sleepy, no-pain, no-motivation close is the textbook under-fueling signature. A mid-race walk-and-drink at mile 10 gave noticeable relief — more confirmation the tank, not the legs, was the limiter.
- **Root cause (Paul's words):** *"Hard to convince myself to take gels when my HR is above 155, nearly impossible when it's above 160."* At HM/MP effort HR sits squarely in 150–165, so the standing every-30-min plan collides with effort-suppressed appetite and most fuel never goes in. The B2B note ("a third gel would have been hard to do") was the early warning of this same effect — now confirmed under a full 97-min race.
- **Why this is the #1 marathon priority (not the time gate):** a 3:30 marathon is ~3.5 hr and 2× today's duration. Glycogen covers ~90–120 min; after that, intake decides whether mile 18–26 happens or unravels. **On today's one-gel-in-97-min pattern, a marathon bonks hard around mile 18–20.** This must be solved in the back-half build, and it outranks any pace target.

**Fixes to train over the next 5 weeks (each long run is now a fueling-execution rehearsal, not just a gut-tolerance one):**

1. **Caffeine gels (Paul's own idea — adopt it).** PF30 Caffeine (+100 mg) is already in the kit for late-race kicks. Caffeine **lowers perceived effort**, which directly attacks the "can't make myself fuel at high HR" wall — and gives a late-race lift exactly when motivation cratered today (the final 4 mi). Plan: at least one caffeine gel positioned for the back third. **Train it first** — never debut caffeine timing/quantity on race day.
2. **Fuel *before* HR climbs, not during.** Same logic as the fast-finish pre-load rule: take gels early and on flat/easy stretches before the effort (and HR) ramps, banking calories while appetite still cooperates. Front-load the schedule so fewer gels come due in the 160-HR zone.
3. **Practice intake *at effort* deliberately.** Easy-pace fueling doesn't transfer — the suppression is effort-driven. On MP/threshold blocks in long runs, force a gel mid-surge on purpose to desensitize the "can't eat at this HR" reflex. Treat taking a gel at HR 160 as a trainable skill.
4. **Consider a higher-HR-friendly format.** Gels may be the hardest thing to choke down at 160 BPM. Test a **carb drink mix sipped from the handheld** (e.g. PF/Maurten drink mix, or the existing C30 mix) as a steady low-effort intake that doesn't require the psychological "commit to a whole gel" moment — small sips spread the carb in without the all-at-once hurdle. Could supplement or partly replace gels for the high-HR stretches.
5. **Decouple the side-stitch loop.** Today's descent **side stitch** → "nervous to eat/drink" → deeper deficit was a vicious cycle. The stitch itself is a breathing/mechanical issue, not a fueling one — but its *consequence* was under-fueling. **Stitch management (separate from fueling):** controlled belly breathing, exhale on the foot-strike opposite the stitch side, shorten stride and stay tall on descents, and **avoid a large gel/fluid bolus right before a downhill** (a full stomach + descent jostling is a classic stitch trigger — favor small sips over big gulps on/just before descents). **Fueling rule when a stitch hits:** keep taking small, steady sips rather than abandoning intake entirely — front-loaded fuel + steady sipping keeps the tank topped so a stitch doesn't get to start the deficit spiral.

### C30 packaging — learned May 14, 2026 (historical, drove the brand switch)

The C30 gel tear notch is unreliable. On the May 14 Thursday rehearsal the gel was hard to open and Paul nearly cut his mouth using teeth. The C30 drink mix wasn't the problem — only the gel pouch. This packaging failure was the deciding factor in switching to Maurten on May 14.

**General fueling-procedure rules (apply to any gel, including PF30):**

- **Two-hand pull only at pace.** Never teeth. If hands are wet/sweaty, dry on shirt first.
- **Pre-check race-day gels at home** the night before — pull-test each pouch's tear notch with a one-handed dry-run tear. If a gel doesn't pop open easy, set it aside.
- **Carry a backup option** (drink mix in a flask) in case a gel still fails mid-race.

**Existing C30 stock:** the drink mix is still good — usable as an emergency pre-race carbohydrate source or for easy-day fueling. C30 gels can be retired (or used as last-resort training-day calories where stopping to open them isn't a problem).

## V. Metabolic Management for the CEO Schedule

- **Stress & cortisol:** High work/construction stress elevates cortisol, which inhibits recovery. Maintain consistent sleep hygiene and prioritize carbohydrate intake on high-stress days to prevent muscle breakdown.
- **Heavy day surplus:** On heavy lifting days (Wednesdays at Cañada College), increase total caloric intake by 300–500 kcal to support muscle protein synthesis.
