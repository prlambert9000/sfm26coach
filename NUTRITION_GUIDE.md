# Performance Nutrition & Metabolic Programming Guide

Pescatarian-aware nutritional framework for a 155 lb (70 kg) athlete with a 58 VO2 max, focused on structural repair, inflammatory management, and glycogen optimization for the SF Marathon 2026.

## I. Macronutrient Targets for High-Volume Training

| Nutrient | Daily Target | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 125–140 g | Eggs, dairy, seafood for leucine and recovery |
| Carbohydrates | 350–500 g | Scale up during peak weeks; complex carbs and hydrogels |
| Fats | 60–80 g | Omega-3s (salmon, sardines) for joint inflammation |

## II. Timing the 6 AM Training Window

- **Pre-workout (5:30 AM):** Quick-digesting carbohydrate — half banana, small toast, or 20 g carb gel — to top off liver glycogen.
- **Immediate post-workout (7:15 AM):** 30 g+ high-leucine protein. Examples: 4 large eggs, or 1.5 cups Greek yogurt with berries.
- **Nightly repair:** 20 g casein-rich protein (cottage cheese or milk) before bed for overnight tissue synthesis.

## III. Pescatarian Anti-Inflammatory Protocol

To manage patellofemoral inflammation, dietary intervention targets systemic inflammation:

- **SMASH fish 3×/week:** Sardines, mackerel, anchovies, salmon, herring. High EPA/DHA dampens inflammation in the patellar fat pad.
- **Collagen co-factors:** High vitamin C (bell peppers, citrus, broccoli) alongside protein meals to support tendon health.
- **Hydration & electrolytes:** Aim for 1000 mg sodium/L during high-sweat 6 AM sessions. Magnesium-rich dairy to prevent cramping.

## IV. Gut Training for Race Day (70–80 g Carb/Hour target)

Goal: train the gut to comfortably absorb **70–80 g/hr** by peak weeks. **Target band revised down from 90 g/hr on June 7, 2026** — see rationale below.

**Race-day carb target — why 70–80, not 90 (set June 7, 2026):** The 90 g/hr ceiling is real physiology — glucose/maltodextrin saturates one gut transporter (SGLT1) at ~60 g/hr, and fructose adds ~30 g/hr via a second (GLUT5), which is why PF30's 1:0.8 malto:fructose blend can exceed 60 cleanly. **But the dose-response is curved, not linear, and the marginal gain from 75→90 is small.** For a ~3.5-hr (3:30 goal) marathon: glycogen covers the first ~90–120 min, after which intake matters — but the big benefit is in the 30→60 jump (bonk insurance), a meaningful-but-smaller gain 60→75, and a marginal one 75→90 that mostly buys insurance for miles 22–26. For a 3:30 runner whose #1 constraint is **knee durability, not a podium**, forcing 90 isn't worth the GI gamble. **The real reason to push the higher end in training is to earn the *option*** — the gut adapts over weeks, so train ~75–80 until it's boring, then race-day sit comfortably at 70–80 with margin to dial up if the day feels great. Net: don't chase a precise number; the difference between 60 and 80 is seconds-to-a-couple-minutes and late-race comfort, not whether 3:30 happens. **90 g/hr stays only as an optional stretch if 25-min spacing feels effortless — never a requirement.**

**Chosen fueling system (RE-LOCKED June 21, 2026 — back to Maurten; supersedes the June 7 PF30 lock):** **Maurten Gel 100 + Gel 100 CAF 100** (the Mix Box: 6 non-caf + 6 caf gels Paul bought Jun 21). **Why the switch back, post-Presidio:** (1) the Presidio HM exposed that Paul's #1 limiter is *getting any gel down at high HR* (couldn't fuel above HR ~155–160), and Maurten's **hydrogel format is firmer / non-syrup and specifically markets "goes down easy even when breathing hard"** — directly aimed at his problem; (2) **he raced on Maurten last year with zero GI issues** — so this is a *return to a personally proven product*, not a novel race-day experiment (the usual "never debut on race day" rule is relaxed because it's not new to him); (3) the **CAF 100 gels deliver the caffeine lever** we identified as a fix (100 mg → blunts perceived effort + late-race mental sharpness, attacking the "can't make myself fuel at high HR" wall). PF30's history was clean — it was never the GI problem — but Maurten better targets the *behavioral/high-HR-intake* problem and Paul has proven tolerance, so it's the right tool now. **Like PF30, Maurten gels are low-sodium** — sodium still comes from LMNT in the handheld (~½ packet ≈ 500 mg per ~16–20 oz, ~1000 mg sodium/L), never the gel.

**The honest caveat — Maurten is not a silver bullet.** The Presidio failure was *as much behavioral/appetite + the side-stitch fear* as it was texture. The firmer hydrogel removes the "syrupy and hard to swallow at effort" excuse and the caffeine helps perceived effort — but Paul still has to **train the act of fueling at HR 155–165.** The product change buys an easier vehicle; the skill still has to be rehearsed in the W8–W10 long runs.

**Note the dose change: Maurten Gel 100 = 25 g vs PF30's 30 g.** That's 5 g less per gel, so the same g/hr needs slightly tighter spacing (or a drink-mix top-up):

**Standing protocol (re-set Jun 21 for 25 g gels):** take **1 gel at T−5→0** (right before start), then **1 gel every ~25 min** = ~60 g/hr baseline. **Spacing → g/hr (25 g gels):** every 30 min = 50 g/hr · every 25 min = **60 g/hr** · every 20 min = 75 g/hr. To reach the 70–80 race-day band: either gels every 20 min, **or** gels every 25–30 min **+ Maurten Drink Mix sips** to close the gap (Maurten's own guidance: stack gel + drink mix for 80–100 g/hr). For Paul specifically, the **gel-every-25-min + drink-mix-sips** combo is likely the better fit — fewer high-HR "swallow a whole gel" moments, more continuous low-effort carb.

- **W8 (deload) — re-introduce Maurten, easy:** 1 gel every 25–30 min on the Sun 10-miler. Confirm the hydrogel sits as well as it did last year. ~50–60 g/hr, no pressure.
- **W9 (60–70 g/hr):** gel every 25 min on the 16-miler; **practice taking the gel mid-MP-block at effort** (the Presidio fix). Add drink-mix sips if reaching for 70.
- **W10 (race-day band, 70–80 g/hr) — full dress rehearsal on the 20-miler:** gel every ~20–25 min + drink mix to hit 75–80, **including a CAF 100 in the back third** to rehearse the caffeine timing and confirm it helps the late-race push. This is the rehearsal that matters most — it mirrors race fueling exactly.
- **Caffeine plan:** 1 CAF 100 to open isn't needed for a morning marathon (already caffeinated from coffee); the higher-value use is **1–2 CAF 100 in the back half** (e.g., ~mi 16 and ~mi 20–22 of the marathon) for the perceived-effort + focus lift when fueling-willpower craters. Cap 3 caf gels/day. **Test the exact timing on the W10 20-miler — not race day.**

**Maurten Drink Mix — get the 160, not the 320 (decided Jun 21):** Drink Mix **160** = 40 g carb + 210 mg sodium / packet; **320** = 80 g carb (double-concentrated) "for race-day intensity." Paul's role for the drink mix is a **supplement to the gels, not the primary carb** — gels at every ~25 min already give ~60 g/hr, and he only needs +10–20 g/hr of top-up to reach the 70–80 band. **The 160 is the right concentration for that;** the 320 stacked on top of full gel cadence would push past 100 g/hr (GI-risk territory) and is more hypertonic (slower emptying — the opposite of what we want for the high-HR problem). Use the 320 only if drink mix ever becomes the *primary* carb source (it won't here).

**The 414 ml handheld vs. Maurten's "mix with exactly 500 ml" — real constraint, the ratio is the hydrogel.** Maurten is emphatic the powder-to-water ratio is non-negotiable — it's what forms the hydrogel; **over-concentrating (a full 41 g packet in 414 ml ≈ 21% too strong) risks breaking the hydrogel AND making it hypertonic** (more GI distress, exactly the problem we're solving). **Fix (pick one):** (a) **best/simplest** — mix a full packet in *exactly 500 ml* in a shaker, fill the 414 ml handheld from it, drink-or-toss the leftover ~86 ml (handheld then carries ~34 g carb at the correct ratio); or (b) buy a cheap **500 ml soft flask** so the mix-and-carry volume matches the packet and the math disappears (worth it given the system rebuild). **Avoid** trying to measure ~83% of a packet into 414 ml — imprecise and fiddly.

**Do NOT add LMNT to the Maurten Drink Mix bottle.** Maurten keeps the drink salt-minimal *by design* ("engineered for optimal carb absorption"); dumping ½ LMNT's salt load into it goes against the non-negotiable formulation and can disrupt the hydrogel. **Keep carbs and electrolytes in separate vessels:** Maurten Drink Mix (carb + its own 210 mg sodium) in one, and if more sodium is needed, take it separately (salt caps, or a second plain-water+LMNT source). Note the 160 alone runs ~420 mg sodium/L — *below* the ~1000 mg/L target and below Paul's proven ½-LMNT setup; fine for cool low-sweat Bay mornings, but for a warm marathon plan supplemental sodium. **Dial the exact carb + electrolyte architecture in the W8–W9 long-run rehearsals before locking the race-day setup.**

### Marathon race-day fluid/fuel architecture (draft, set Jun 21, 2026 — refine through the rehearsals)

**The handheld is a carb vessel, NOT the water supply.** A 500 ml handheld can't hydrate a 3.5-hr marathon and isn't meant to — the SF Marathon's aid stations (~every 2–3 mi ≈ every 15–20 min) are the hydration source. Division of labor: **self-carry the carb (aid stations don't stock Maurten); get water from the course.**

- **Hydration:** aid-station water, to thirst.
- **Carb backbone:** Maurten Gel 100 every ~20–25 min — **no water chaser needed** (the hydrogel is designed to be taken without it); **CAF 100 in the back third** (~mi 16 + ~mi 20–22).
- **Easy-sip supplement:** Drink Mix 160 in the handheld (ideally a collapsible **soft flask** — sip down over the first half where forcing gels at effort is hardest, then pocket/toss it; optional spare DM packet to remix mid-race for a back-half sip option).
- **Total:** ~8–9 gels + 1 drink mix ≈ **70 g/hr**, in the 70–80 band.
- **Electrolytes:** **none engineered.** Paul is not a salty sweater and has never run low; SF July is cool/foggy with an early start (low sweat/sodium loss). DM's 210 mg + aid-station electrolyte cups cover it. **No LMNT in the race, never mixed into the Maurten.** **Warm-day backup: SaltStick Electrolyte Fastchews (chewable, chosen Jun 21 over GU Roctane capsules)** — carried in a pocket, taken only if needed. **Chewable over capsule deliberately: no water required to take them**, which fits Paul's high-HR-intake problem (a capsule needs a swallow + water — the exact friction we're avoiding); ~100 mg sodium per serving (check per-chew count), 1–2 as needed.

### Self-supported long-run rehearsal setup (Paul's method, Jun 21 — simulates the race exactly)

Training long runs from the door have **no aid stations**, so to rehearse the race architecture faithfully: **carry the handheld (Drink Mix 160) + gels exactly as race day, PLUS a hydration vest filled with water-only to play the role of the aid stations.** Drink the vest water to thirst (standing in for course water), keep the handheld strictly for carb sips, and take gels on the race cadence. This reproduces the exact race split — carb self-carried, water "from the course" — on an unsupported route, and lets the W8–W10 long runs dress-rehearse the real fueling without routing around fountains. (Bonus: vest-vs-handheld carry feel, and whether the firmer Maurten goes down at MP-block HR, both get tested under race-like load.)

**Fast-finish fueling rule (learned June 7):** when a long run has an embedded MP/threshold close, the back-half gel often comes due *during* the surge and is easy to skip under pace focus (Paul missed the 90-min gel on June 7 for exactly this reason — a focus call, not GI). **Pre-load it:** take the gel that would land mid-surge a few minutes early, *before* the fast finish begins, so the feeding is banked before attention narrows to pace.

### Presidio HM — the high-HR fueling problem (learned June 21, 2026 — THE binding constraint for the marathon)

The Presidio Half (Pivot test) exposed the single most important fueling fact of the build, and it is **not** a gut-tolerance problem — it's a **behavioral/appetite-at-effort** problem. **GI has never been the limiter; getting fuel *in* at race HR is.**

- **What happened:** plan was 3 carried / 2 taken (start + ~mi 6.5). **Actual = one gel, at ~65 min.** A **side stitch** (under the right ribs, on the Presidio→bridge descent — an ETAP-type stitch, NOT a muscle cramp; it resolved on its own) made him gun-shy about eating/drinking for the back half, which compounded the appetite suppression → a glycogen-flat final ~4 miles: *"tired, hard to push, almost sleepy, hard to stay motivated… not pain, just not wanting to push."* That sleepy, no-pain, no-motivation close is the textbook under-fueling signature. A mid-race walk-and-drink at mile 10 gave noticeable relief — more confirmation the tank, not the legs, was the limiter.
- **Root cause (Paul's words):** *"Hard to convince myself to take gels when my HR is above 155, nearly impossible when it's above 160."* At HM/MP effort HR sits squarely in 150–165, so the standing every-30-min plan collides with effort-suppressed appetite and most fuel never goes in. The B2B note ("a third gel would have been hard to do") was the early warning of this same effect — now confirmed under a full 97-min race.
- **Why this is the #1 marathon priority (not the time gate):** a 3:30 marathon is ~3.5 hr and 2× today's duration. Glycogen covers ~90–120 min; after that, intake decides whether mile 18–26 happens or unravels. **On today's one-gel-in-97-min pattern, a marathon bonks hard around mile 18–20.** This must be solved in the back-half build, and it outranks any pace target.

**Fixes to train over the next 5 weeks (each long run is now a fueling-execution rehearsal, not just a gut-tolerance one):**

1. **Caffeine gels (Paul's own idea — DONE, bought Jun 21).** Paul switched back to Maurten and bought the Mix Box including **Gel 100 CAF 100 (100 mg caffeine)**. Caffeine **lowers perceived effort**, which directly attacks the "can't make myself fuel at high HR" wall — and gives a late-race lift exactly when motivation cratered at Presidio (the final 4 mi). Plan: 1–2 CAF 100 in the back third (e.g., marathon ~mi 16 + ~mi 20–22), cap 3/day. **Train the timing on the W10 20-miler first** — never debut caffeine quantity/timing on race day.
2. **Fuel *before* HR climbs, not during.** Same logic as the fast-finish pre-load rule: take gels early and on flat/easy stretches before the effort (and HR) ramps, banking calories while appetite still cooperates. Front-load the schedule so fewer gels come due in the 160-HR zone.
3. **Practice intake *at effort* deliberately.** Easy-pace fueling doesn't transfer — the suppression is effort-driven. On MP/threshold blocks in long runs, force a gel mid-surge on purpose to desensitize the "can't eat at this HR" reflex. Treat taking a gel at HR 160 as a trainable skill.
4. **Consider a higher-HR-friendly format.** Gels may be the hardest thing to choke down at 160 BPM. Test a **carb drink mix sipped from the handheld** (e.g. PF/Maurten drink mix, or the existing C30 mix) as a steady low-effort intake that doesn't require the psychological "commit to a whole gel" moment — small sips spread the carb in without the all-at-once hurdle. Could supplement or partly replace gels for the high-HR stretches.
5. **Decouple the side-stitch loop.** Today's descent **side stitch** → "nervous to eat/drink" → deeper deficit was a vicious cycle. The stitch itself is a breathing/mechanical issue, not a fueling one — but its *consequence* was under-fueling. **Stitch management (separate from fueling):** controlled belly breathing, exhale on the foot-strike opposite the stitch side, shorten stride and stay tall on descents, and **avoid a large gel/fluid bolus right before a downhill** (a full stomach + descent jostling is a classic stitch trigger — favor small sips over big gulps on/just before descents). **Fueling rule when a stitch hits:** keep taking small, steady sips rather than abandoning intake entirely — front-loaded fuel + steady sipping keeps the tank topped so a stitch doesn't get to start the deficit spiral.

### C30 packaging — learned May 14, 2026 (historical, drove the brand switch)

The C30 gel tear notch is unreliable. On the May 14 Thursday rehearsal the gel was hard to open and Paul nearly cut his mouth using teeth. The C30 drink mix wasn't the problem — only the gel pouch. This packaging failure was the deciding factor in switching to Maurten on May 14.

**General fueling-procedure rules (apply to any gel, including PF30):**

- **Two-hand pull only at pace.** Never teeth. If hands are wet/sweaty, dry on shirt first.
- **Pre-check race-day gels at home** the night before — pull-test each pouch's tear notch with a one-handed dry-run tear. If a gel doesn't pop open easy, set it aside.
- **Carry a backup option** (drink mix in a flask) in case a gel still fails mid-race.

**Existing C30 stock:** the drink mix is still good — usable as an emergency pre-race carbohydrate source or for easy-day fueling. C30 gels can be retired (or used as last-resort training-day calories where stopping to open them isn't a problem).

## V. Metabolic Management for the CEO Schedule

- **Stress & cortisol:** High work/construction stress elevates cortisol, which inhibits recovery. Maintain consistent sleep hygiene and prioritize carbohydrate intake on high-stress days to prevent muscle breakdown.
- **Heavy day surplus:** On heavy lifting days (Wednesdays at Cañada College), increase total caloric intake by 300–500 kcal to support muscle protein synthesis.
